You lose the AGI argument the moment you skip the definition. Nvidia's CEO won it with 8 words. Which 8?

March 2026. Lex Fridman asks Jensen Huang how long until an AI can start, grow and run a tech company worth more than a billion dollars.

Huang's answer: "I think we've achieved AGI."

Then come the eight words. "You said billion, and you didn't say forever."

His point: the AI only has to build something that reaches a billion dollars once. It does not have to keep it alive.

Commentators noticed right away that few AI researchers define AGI this way. The usual meaning is much harder: a system that matches people across almost every kind of thinking work.

So those are the eight words. By his definition, AGI is here. By the usual one, it is not.
